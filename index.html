    1. 01 · Brief

      "Make some thumbnails for this video."

      A designer hears make it look good. The real questions come first: what does the video promise, who is buying, and what makes that person click?

    2. 02 · The video

      The thumbnail makes the promise. The first 30 seconds keep it.

      Every concept is tied to a line actually said on camera, so the click never turns into a bounce.

    3. 03 · Audience

      Viewers are not buyers.

      990 comments across 9 channels showed who actually watches. The title carries the words people search; the thumbnail shows the outcome the buyer wants.

    4. 04 · Outliers

      What already wins, and why.

      207 videos that did at least 2.5× their channel's normal, across 39 channels. The winners put proof in the frame. Then the same format, borrowed from other niches.

    5. 05 · Patterns

      Measured, not guessed.

      140 thumbnails from the same channels, 70 outliers against 70 normal videos. The heavy, glowing composite the niche defaults to is what the normal videos do. Worked but simple wins.

    6. 06 · Sketch

      The idea on paper before any pixels.

      If a concept doesn't read as a stick figure, no amount of polish will save it.

    7. 07 · Reference

      Steal the structure, never the look.

      One proven layout from a different niche. The composition carries over; the face, colours and story are this video's own.

    8. 08 · Polish

      From raw to finished.

      Cut-out, grade, type. Keep scrolling to see the frame change.

    9. 09 · Phone test

      Judged at feed size, among its competitors.

      Most clicks happen on a phone. If it doesn't stand out next to the videos it competes with, it goes back.

    10. 10 · Three bets

      Three angles, each with its own title.

      Proof, a contrarian claim, and a new behaviour. Ready to A/B test, with the reasoning for each.
